## The dataset

The Satcom Pathways deals tracker holds **36 entries** dated January 2025 to October 2026: 10 from 2025 and 26 from 2026. Each entry comes from a public announcement and links to its source. The tracker covers satellite communications, telecom and rural connectivity. It leaves out Earth observation, weather, navigation and launch.

Only two entries have a disclosed dollar value: Satlyt's $8M seed round and the $1M feasibility study for an East African communications satellite. This review counts deals rather than adding up money.

## Four things the data shows

**1. Regulation is where most of the activity is.** Licence decisions, a court ruling and a tender make up 11 of the 36 entries. Starlink appears in 8 of them, and the outcomes differ by country. DR Congo, Seychelles, the Central African Republic, Uganda and Côte d'Ivoire granted licences. Namibia's regulator refused one in March 2026. In September 2026 Senegal's Supreme Court provisionally suspended Starlink's licence after a challenge from Sonatel. In South Africa, ICASA said in May 2026 that alternatives to its local ownership rules would need a change in law.

**2. Direct-to-device has reached signed operator deals.** All three direct-to-device entries are agreements with African mobile operators: Airtel Africa and SpaceX for Starlink Direct to Cell across its 14 markets (December 2025), MTN Zambia's field test with Starlink, described as the first in Africa (March 2026), and AXIAN Telecom (Yas) with AST SpaceMobile (March 2026).

**3. Governments are building their own satellite capacity.** Nigeria approved NigComSat-2A and 2B on 22 August 2026, and the satellite build and gateway contracts followed within four weeks. In April 2026 Uganda, Kenya, Rwanda and South Sudan agreed to fund a feasibility study for a shared communication and broadcasting satellite. South Africa launched the state-backed BrainSAT with Space42 in June 2026. Angola is selling Angosat-2 capacity beyond its borders, starting with a pilot agreement with Telecom Namibia.

**4. Amazon Leo's African agreements are all with local partners.** Its agreements are with Vanu for rural mobile sites, Vodacom for cell-site backhaul and Herotel for consumer broadband in South Africa. Separate entries record Nigeria's landing permit and Amazon Kuiper Kenya's application for an International Gateway Operator licence.

## Where the countries are

Nigeria and South Africa appear in 5 entries each. Namibia, Kenya and Angola appear in 4 each, as do pan-African deals.

## Limits

The tracker is built by hand from public announcements, so it under-counts private and undisclosed deals. Counts describe announcement activity, not market size.
